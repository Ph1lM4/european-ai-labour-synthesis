## 5. European procurement-vs-R&D split

| Metric | Value | Quote |
|---|---|---|
| Investment share of European budgets 2024 | 30% (up from 15% a decade ago) | "the average allocation to investment within European Defense budgets is now 30% compared to 15% a decade ago" |
| R&D share of investment | "still around 2%" | "R&D budgets haven't actually increased as an allocation. It's still around 2%" |
| Recapitalisation share | up to 40%+ in some countries | "we're seeing some countries spending in excess of 40% of their budget on recapitalization" |

**For Lens 5(a) scoring:** Investment-side surge is real but heavily procurement-loaded. R&D underspend = capacity-building lag = bandwidth-tax compounds over time (current tank-spend doesn't build long-term industrial capacity).

## 6. Force-industrial constraint quotes (Lens 5(a) bandwidth-tax mechanism)

> "the major concern in Europe is the sustainability of current defense spending increases at a time of significant fiscal pressures across the continent" — Bastian Giegerich

> "potentially elements of social care or pensions are going to have to be impacted to continue to fund defense" — Fenella McGerty

> "the level of growth that we've seen in European spending in 2024 is going to be incredibly difficult to maintain without some sacrifices... it took 10 years to get within touching distance of the 2% and a lot of that growth within the last two years so it took an invasion of a country on Europe's continent for that pivot to happen" — McGerty

**Methodology note for Lens 5(a):** these IISS-analyst quotes attest to the bandwidth-competition mechanism that Lens 5(a) theorises. The mechanism is named explicitly by domain experts: defence growth requires sacrificing social care / pensions / R&D / readiness. This is direct support for Polycrisis Drag's institutional-bandwidth framing — independent of Tooze.

## 7. Workforce and recruitment constraints

> "challenges remain notably around workforce shortages, supply chain bottlenecks and still nascent order books" — on European defence-industrial uplift

> "skilled workers is definitely a challenge" — Tom (re: capacity-to-deliver)

> "supply chain that shrunk to meet a reduced demand that's definitely a challenge" — Tom

> "in Europe... [there's] still skepticism about the value of defense and getting funding for defense companies or even permission from local governments to build factories is very difficult in some places" — Tom

**For Task 3 reshoring component (b):** Defence-industrial reshoring is constrained by labour and local-permitting bottlenecks, not just capital. This caveats simple "defence growth → reshoring jobs" causal chain.
