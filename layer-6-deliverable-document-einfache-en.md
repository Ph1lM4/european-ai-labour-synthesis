## What we did

We looked at 36 European countries. We asked one question. Can each country handle the changes that AI brings to work?

We tested 5 things in every country. We call them "lenses."

Lens 1 checks how fast jobs change. It also checks how fast a country can help workers find new jobs.

Lens 2 checks if older workers retire fast enough to leave room for new jobs.

Lens 3 checks who is hit hardest by AI changes.

Lens 4 checks if a country faces other crises at the same time. War, climate, and migration are examples.

Lens 5 checks how strong a country's institutions are. Schools, training programs, and laws all count.

We also tested 7 future situations. We call them "scenarios."

Then we sorted the countries into 4 groups. We call these groups "fragility classes." A class shows how at-risk a country is.

## What we found

9 countries are stable under stress. They are: Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Belgium, France, Luxembourg, the Netherlands. We call these Class 1.

9 countries are fragile. They look stable today. But under one bad scenario they fall into trouble. They are: Austria, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Bulgaria, Switzerland, Germany, Spain, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Romania. We call these Class 2.

15 countries are at risk of failure. They are already in trouble under everyday conditions. They are: Cyprus, Czechia, Estonia, Greece, Croatia, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Portugal, Slovenia, Slovakia, the United Kingdom. We call these Class 3.

3 countries are in active crisis. They are: North Macedonia, Serbia, Turkey. We call these Class 4.

That gives us 9 + 9 + 15 + 3 = 36 countries.

We also found that older workers do not save Europe. Many people hope that retiring older workers will leave room for new jobs. The numbers say no. The maximum we found was about 26 percent. We needed at least 80 percent for that hope to work. No country reached 80 percent.

But the failure is not the same in every kind of job. Office and admin jobs lose 60 to 80 percent of their tasks to AI. Only 3.5 million older workers retire from these jobs by 2030. So 5 to 10 million people lose work faster than retirement makes room. Care, trade, and health jobs lose only 5 to 15 percent of their tasks to AI. But about 12 million older workers retire from these jobs. So 8 to 12 million jobs stay empty. Italy shows the problem most clearly. In 2025, more people left Italy than came in. The number was minus 485,823.

## Why retraining is not enough

About 7.55 million workers in Europe need deep retraining by 2035. But the training system can retrain only about 450,000 new people per year for AI changes. So Europe needs about 15 years. AI changes jobs in 1 to 3 years. The training system needs 5 to 9 years to react. This gap is the main reason 15 countries are in Class 3.

## What "stable" means

We tested every country with two kinds of rules. The first rule was strict. The second rule was softer.

The strict rule says a country is stable only if no scenario makes things worse than the baseline scenario. The softer rule says a country is stable if its situation stays close to the baseline. The softer rule also requires that no scenario pushes the country into the worst group.

Under the strict rule, 0 countries are stable. Even Norway fails. Even Sweden fails.

Under the softer rule, 9 countries are stable.

So no European country is fully safe. The 9 stable countries are stable under softer rules only. They are not stable under strict rules.

This is an important warning. Reports often say that Europe will manage the change well. But the strict test shows that no European country is unconditionally safe. The 9 countries are conditionally safe. They are safe if the world does not test them strictly.

## A special warning for 8 countries

8 countries face an extra risk. They are Belgium, Germany, Denmark, Finland, France, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden. These countries protect their workers strongly. But the strong protection creates a different problem.

The 4 Nordic countries are exposed because trade is changing. Some industries may move out.

The 4 other countries are exposed because the United Kingdom protects workers less. Money and AI investment can move there. Workers stay safe at home, but the regional economy can lose investment.

This warning is about money flow, not job loss. It is a separate problem with a separate solution.

## What can change things

The situation is not fixed forever. Every country can make choices to improve its future. Europe as a whole can also act.

Government training programs are one tool. We call them "Active Labour Market Policies" or ALMP. They are training and job-search programs. They help workers move to new jobs. Strong programs protect a country in bad scenarios.

EU money is a second tool. The EU has a 7-year budget. We call it the MFF. The MFF can fund worker support, schools, and crisis response. How the EU spends this budget changes the result.

Schools and universities are a third tool. Schools teach the skills people need for new jobs. Universities train high-skill workers. Both help countries adjust to AI changes.

Rules and laws are a fourth tool. Laws control how AI is used at work. Strong rules can protect workers. Weak rules can leave workers exposed.

Money the government has free is a fifth tool. Some countries have more money to spend in a crisis. Others have less. Money matters more under bad scenarios.

This brief tells you what is happening today. It does not tell you what to do about it.

What countries can do to improve their class is the topic of a different document. We call it Part 7 of this project. It is being prepared.

So please remember. The class a country sits in today is not fixed. It is a snapshot of present conditions. Countries can move between classes if they make different choices.

## Glossary

- **ALMP** (Active Labour Market Policies). Training and job-search programs run by governments.
- **Cascade.** A chain of failures. One problem leads to the next.
- **Corridor.** A risk band. We have 3 corridors. C1 is the safest. C3 is the riskiest.
- **Displacement.** When a worker loses a job because a machine or new method does the work.
- **Fragility class.** A risk group for whole countries. We have 4 classes. Class 1 is the safest. Class 4 is in crisis.
- **Lens.** One way to test a country. We use 5 lenses.
- **MFF.** The 7-year budget of the European Union. It pays for many EU activities.
- **Post-growth.** An economy where total output stops growing. Old patterns of recovery work less well there.
- **Regime.** An economic situation. We use 3 regimes: growth, slow growth, post-growth.
- **Reinstatement.** When new jobs replace old jobs as technology changes. The historical pattern.
- **Robust.** Stable under stress. Survives a hard test.
- **Scenario.** One possible future. We tested 7 scenarios.
- **Secular stagnation.** A long period of slow economic growth.
- **Squeeze flag.** A warning about capital-flight risk. Different from worker displacement.
- **Structural bias.** When numbers seem reliable but the method tilts the result.
- **Zone A jobs.** Office and admin jobs. AI can do most of these tasks.
- **Zone C jobs.** Health, care, and trade jobs. AI can do only a few of these tasks.
